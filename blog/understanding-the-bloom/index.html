Understanding the Pour-Over Bloom

What is the 'bloom' phase and why is it crucial for a perfect cup?

2026-05-26

What is the Bloom?

When hot water touches fresh coffee grounds, a rapid release of carbon dioxide occurs. This is called the “bloom.” It looks like the coffee bed is bubbling and expanding.

This gas is a byproduct of the roasting process, trapped inside the cellular structure of the bean. If you don’t allow it to escape, the gas will repel water during your main pour, leading to uneven extraction.

How to Bloom Properly

  1. Water Amount: Pour twice to three times the weight of your coffee. If using 15g of coffee, pour 30g - 45g of water.
  2. Agitation: Ensure all dry spots are saturated. You can swirl the dripper gently or use a spoon to excavate.
  3. Time: Wait 30 to 45 seconds. Watch the bubbles. Once the bubbling slows down significantly, you’re ready to proceed with your main pour.
